## Gnat

“Gnat! On your six!” someone screamed in his ear.

“I see it,” Gnat yelled, and he twisted his stick, sending his Challenger into a tight spiral. The enemy craft shot past and in front of him, wobbled as its pilot tried to see where Gnat had gone, then exploded in a bright blue ball as Gnat’s plasma torpedoes tore through it.

“BLUE TEAM WINS!” The Challenge Zone faded away, into gridlines and then into the calm sky blue of the lobby. An angry red icon hung at the corner of Gnat’s vision as a group of improbable avatars tumbled together, slapping backs and talking scrag.

*You have one playtime credit remaining,* the icon warned. Gnat sighed. A Champions of the Challenge Zone StrikeMatch took two credits. He was done for the day. The damp chill of the shipping container was already creeping back in, clawing at the corners of the VR rig strapped to his head. Gnat smelled poverty and dirty water. He lingered in the lobby and ignored the red icon. A few more minutes, and then he would deal with the real world.

“Gnat!” The avatar that bounced in front of him was some kind of imp or devil. “That was some tight flying, man.” The name New_Deviles hung over its head. “You want to flist? I’d fly on your team again any time, esé: you’re way better than the clones-and-drones I’m usually matched with.”

“Don’t do that,” said Gnat, waving New_Deviles back. “Don’t talk about clones like that.”

“Like what?”

“Like they’re machines. Like drones.”

“D00d,” said New_Deviles. “They’re androids.”

“Yeah,” chimed in another avatar, this one a sleek Nipponese-styled fantasy robot. It tapped its chest, a ringing metal sound. “Androids are machines, like, by definition.”

“I should know, right?” laughed New_Deviles. “My mom works for Jinteki.”

“Shut it, scrub.” Gnat hissed. “You’re talking about my mom and dad, okay?”

“Woah,” laughed New_Deviles. “I don’t even know where to start. Like, that’s first of all literally impossible, and I don’t think even legal.”

“I’m adopted, you scrag.”

“Dev, look at his loc-tag,” said the robot. “He’s Brazilian.” Gnat winced. He hadn’t bothered to spoof his location tag for the game. Normally it didn’t matter, but he suddenly didn’t want New_Deviles to know that he lived in one of the poorest favelas in the country.

“You know what?” sneered New_Deviles. “I take it back. I don’t want a clone lover like you on my team.”

Gnat peeled off his haptic glove. The hard metal and damp paper of the floor beneath him broke through the fantasy; a cold reminder of the real world as he fumbled about for his other console. “Yeah, yeah,” he said. His fingers brushed the plastic case of his “work” machine. “You said your mom works for Jinteki, right? What division?”

It was petty, Gnat knew. But he had no playtime credits left, so what else was he going to do?
