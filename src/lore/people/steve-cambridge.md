Steve Cambridge works at SYNC under the name Alec, posing as an incompetent technician. He taps the trunk lines of its routing center and finds a huge encrypted file.

---

“Alec! One more screw-up and your tenure here at SYNC will be very short, you get me?”

“Sorry boss,” Cambridge said. “I didn’t realize.” He smiled brightly, like a dog who just wanted to make his master happy. It was a good bit. Soft-hearted bozos like Sheppard ate it up.

Cambridge shook his head as he passed into the guts of SYNC’s Manta District Master Information Routing Center. It took a surprising amount of effort to play the part of barely-competent techie, but it was worth it for his sweet position in the heart of New Angeles’s Network infrastructure. So far, it had been a great cover for his other ventures.

Minutes later, Cambridge sat in the midst of a humming forest of server spires, his breath misting in the machine-chilled air. He was, technically, done for the day. Or rather, Alec’s duties were all completed, thanks to the assistance of a handful of secretaries he’d previously kitbashed together for this purpose.

How long would Sheppard expect him to be busy back here? Hours, probably. What to do? Might as well put a tap on the trunk lines, he reasoned, and set an AI to sift for anything interesting. Or valuable, which was Cambridge’s favorite flavor of interesting.

Twenty minutes later he was watching connections flicker past him as colored bubbles, grinning all the while. SYNC would never know about the tap unless someone physically opened the panel and looked, and whose job was it to do that? Alec’s. He would have to sneaker-net the data in and out, but that was easily done. Just another windfall from this lucrative—

His eye twitched as he read the bare parameters of recent traffic. There is was—an enormous file, heavily encrypted. Impossible to decrypt in reasonable time with any kind of normal rig.

“Gosh, it’s a good thing I own the entire Network from here,” murmured Cambridge.

“Mister Hardison!” barked Sheppard, tap-tapping down the metal grating of the corridors. “Are you caught up yet?”

Cambridge tried contrition again; it had seemed to work the best before. “Sorry, boss, not quite. Something went wrong with the Set C reboot and I’m trying to run a diagnostic.”

Sheppard was already gone, with no indication she had any idea what Cambridge was doing. But Cambridge didn’t have time to gloat. He was on the cusp of something interesting enough to retire on.
