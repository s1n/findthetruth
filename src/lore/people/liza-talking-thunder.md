## Liza Talking Thunder

“Do you think your granddaughter would be proud of what you’ve done, Congresswoman?” The reporter—Rook was his name—sprawled in his chair across from her. He took up too much space—in her office, in her thoughts. He didn’t care; he held himself as if her attention were his birthright.

She would enjoy throwing that arrogance away.

“Of course she would,” Liza said. “I can count the number of Native congresswomen, in our entire history, on one hand. My legislative accomplishments, on the other hand, are beyond counting. There’s a statue of me in the Twins. I restored the Black Hills to the Lakota Sioux!”

“Most of them,” Rook acknowledged. “But that’s not what I’m talking about, and you know it, Congresswoman Talking Thunder.” He leaned forward, placing his hands on his knees, splaying his elbows out—taking up space. “I’m talking about the backroom deals, the suspicious no-bid contracts your son’s company has acquired, the bribery, the blackmail.”

“Bold claims,” Liza said. “Even libelous.”

“It’s all sourced,” said Rook. “It’s barely even secret, Congresswoman. Your son having a different last name from you is not exactly cloak-and-dagger level stuff.” He pulled out his PAD, one of those chunky biolocked models favored by journos, and held his thumb to the sensor. Her wall screen soon swam with quotes, images, and files building Rook’s case. “I don’t need you to confirm any of this. I’m just giving you an opportunity to comment before I push the article live.”

She didn’t bother to examine any of Rook’s material. “Do you think you’re the first reporter to stumble across this alleged corruption, Mr. Rook?” He paused, his brow furrowing. She suppressed a smile. It wouldn’t do to let the predator out too soon. “If, as you say, it is so easy to uncover, why has no such story been published before now?” She shrugged. “God in Heinlein, I’ve been in Congress over ten years.”

Rook stared. Something glimmered in his eyes. Perhaps he was smarter than she’d given him credit for. Maybe he suspected the hook beneath the worm. “What are you trying to say, Congresswoman?”

“What’s that in your pocket?” she asked. An image flashed through her mind: dead fish, mouth agape, a knife flashing. Rook pulled the memory diamond from his pocket, staring at it. “I wonder how that got there. Perhaps you should scan it.” He touched the interface plastic case to his PAD, and she saw his monolens flare with virt.

“What is—?”

“Oh, dear, Mr. Rook. You’ve been a bad boy. The illicit sexies, those are pretty tame. Who doesn’t have a few skeletons in that particular closet? But the plagiarism? That could be damaging. And the illegal android trafficking? That’s a prison sentence, Mr. Rook.”

“But some of this isn’t even true!” he managed.

She laughed, surprising herself with a sharp bark. “‘True?’ Mr. Rook, do you really think that matters?” She shook her head. “This is the Hill, Mr. Rook. No one here is clean. Not even you.”
