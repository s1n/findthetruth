Exile perched on the edge of a low old-style brick building, somewhere near the
Bahía de Manta, watching the flickering lights of the hoppers. Their lights formed
a pattern as they flew along the strict lanes, rising and falling to their pads.
The web of hopper routes hinted at the complexity of the greater web that was New
Angeles, which sprawled in all directions as far as he could see. He realized it
wasn't really a flow of hoppers he was watching; it was the flow of people. New
Angeles was still a puzzle that he hadn't solved.

The other puzzle he hadn't solved, wouldn't even try, was the one inside his own
head. He was less than a klick from the beach, where the ristie youth would
congregate to play in the Pacific surf. He knew she might be there. He couldn't
bear the thought of her seeing him. He couldn't live much longer without seeing
her. The push and pull inside his own mind was unresolvable.

He jacked in, drowning the distracting pattern of his thoughts in a sea of data.
First he called up a sniffer, letting it detect the data that permeated the air
around him. Gradually it built a map, a network of gleaming light overlaying his
display lenses. A pattern, putting another layer on the puzzle of New Angeles. But
beneath the data were secrets, he knew. The flow of data was just one layer--the
information contained within was another. And the secrets lurking at the heart of
the corp servers, another still. The corps were jealous with their secrets, and
they didn't like questions. She'd told him that, back when--

He jacked out. Distracting himself wasn't working. He couldn't think, couldn't see
the patterns clearly with his head like this. He dropped down to the street,
sliding down a tree-trunk of bundled cables and power lines. Maybe some scavenging
would defrag his mind.

Twenty minutes later he had found most of an Akamatsu Typhoon, a CSI memchip
attached to a slagged PAD, and dinner. He drifted to the edge of the beach,
standing over a busy hopper pad staffed by a cluster of clones. Hoppers touched
down and gaudily clad risties emerged, strutting down towards the beach without a
second look at the clones or their abandoned hoppers.

She wasn't one of them. Of course not. He was being stupid. He turned to go. He
would move, relocate to Quevedo. Away from the distractions. Someplace he could
see the patterns more clearly. He walked further into his exile.
