*A short story about a new accountant’s first day, from **ADN56 — Reign and Reverie**.*

## The Outfit

When Karol reached the new office, he promptly went back outside and checked that he had the suite number right. Maybe he was in the wrong building—this part of ChiLo had so many: short, squat things, a tiny fragment of the size of an ark.

The door opened, and a woman leaned against the frame. “You Karol?” she asked in her retro-styled, pale-grey suit complete with braces and waistcoat. A gold chain ran into one pocket, no doubt where her PAD was stashed. Her jacket was elsewhere, and Karol’s mind stumbled over how broad her shoulders were.

Karol managed a nod and tugged at his own tie. His job in SanSan hadn’t required a suit and tie, but he understood that things got more formal the further east you flew. “Yes,” he stammered. “I’m the new accountant.”

“That’s great,” the woman said, ushering him in. The suite echoed in its emptiness as she led him past old particleboard desks, some with tangles of out-of-date data cords and jacks sprayed across them. “I’m Regan. You’ll meet Alphonse later.” They passed through the front room and entered another open-plan office, a city of empty cubes. “Any questions about the gig?”

“Y-yes,” he stammered, struggling to keep up with Regan’s shoulders-first momentum. “I was wondering about the, uh, cash-only nature of my salary. Of course I see the, ah, substantial advantages vis-à-vis tax declarations, but, well, it’s hard to secure an apartment in the area without a verified source of income, and—”

“Don’t worry about it,” said Regan. “You don’t want to stay in one of those dumps anyway. The Outfit’ll set you up in a nice place.”

“That’s another thing. I’m not sure who’s employing me? My transfer paperwork from BE Finance said something about ChiLo Holdings Ltd., but as far as I can tell, they don’t do anything, and then your message was from Ace Real Estate, which I can’t even confirm exists…”

“You work for the Outfit, now,” Regan said. “Don’t worry about it.” She nudged him to one side as a pair of workers in grey-and-blue jumpsuits walked past, wheeling a long black-wrapped package out on a rattling old cart. “Fellas,” she said. The one in front doffed his hat.

“That’s a job that could be done by a bioroid,” Karol noted. “Save a few bucks.” He swallowed, stammered, cleared his throat. Given how swiftly and efficiently he’d been replaced by a bioroid at BE Finance, he wasn’t eager for Regan to get any ideas.

“Don’t worry about the bioroid thing,” Regan said as she pushed open the door to the corner office—old office, old manual door. “Certain jobs can only be done by a human, you get me? We need an accountant with a certain amount of… creativity.”

Karol stepped into the office, had time to notice the air-gapped terminal on the old, heavy wood desk before he gagged and staggered back out through the door. “What is that smell?”

“Yeah, I guess the chemicals the cleaners use are pretty strong,” Regan admitted. “We’ll leave the door propped, give it a few hours to air out.”

Karol looked away from his office, from the stain on his new rug. His eyes fell on the snub-nosed flechette thrower hanging from Regan’s shoulder holster. “What…” He swallowed. “What happened to the guy who had this job before me?”

Regan clapped one powerful hand on his shoulder and gave a firm squeeze. “Don’t worry about it.”
