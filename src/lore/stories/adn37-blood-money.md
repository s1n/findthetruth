## Blood Money

Just about four dozen drones were in the air and MacPherson still couldn't find this Emelyov
person. He sat on some sort of strut or brace halfway up the ark, enjoying the stiff breeze. He
figured he should probably learn what the hell he was leaning on, given how much joy it had given
him the past few days. He was spending a lot of time here since getting subbed into New Angeles,
running his drones and ignoring the suits.

There was a suit below him now, yelling into the wind. MacPherson grinned down at her and gave a
thumbs-up. That was usually enough to get them to go away. He leaned back down and jacked in. He
let his skulljack hum, swimming through feeds from drones all over the city. He was everywhere,
seeing everything. And there was a lot to see: bounty notices flickering past—someone named Khan
was claiming a bunch of them, unmarked hoppers loading up at a Weyland-owned factory—something
shady there, sec teams rousting *disenfrancistos* from corp-owned territory, more so than usual.
But no sign of Emelyov, not on drone-cam, nothing on NBN's traditional surveillance network.

The strut thrummed and MacPherson straightened up again, jacking out. The damn suit was climbing
up next to him. She squawked and fumbled, buffeted by the wind, and one of her shoes fell off,
blown away in an instant. MacPherson laughed and offered her one meaty arm, pulling her up beside
him. He figured she'd earned it. "What's up?" he asked.

"I would not have to climb out here if you would just answer your damn PAD!" she growled, her
hands white-knuckled as she grabbed at the strut.

"I'm off the books," he said. "Figured if you couldn't ping me on a PAD, no one would be able to
prove you had anything to do with anything I do, see?"

"That sounds like an excuse. Do you have Emelyov or not?"

"At the moment, not," he said. He took a moment to study the suit. She was NBN-pretty, her hair
now thoroughly out of order from the wind, with light brown skin and eyes that she kept scrunched
shut against the wind or vertigo or both. "I got Banquo and Falstaff on station outside her
apartment, but thermal shows no one home. Why do we want her, anyway?"

"She defected from Titan, flipped to Jinteki."

"So?"

"So, she made the swap almost exactly twenty-four hours before the incident." The suit opened her
eyes, stiffened, grabbed MacPherson's arm right below the shoulder, and leaned back again. "We
have a leak that says the crash was an inside job. Even if Emelyov's not involved, Titan has to
be worried that other top talent will jump ship. It's either a story or it's leverage. Emelyov.
Find her." She took a deep breath. MacPherson could feel her not looking down. "Send in Banksy
and Failsafe, or whatever."

"Banquo and Falstaff," he said, and slipped back into full immersion. Sending the drones in
wasn't difficult, it was just very illegal, violating Emelyov's privacy like that. Not that
MacPherson cared.

A red ping flared in the corner of his mind. He called it up, getting Banquo's feed. He could
hear the suit, distantly, talking at him. "What is it?"

"I found what's left of Emelyov," said MacPherson. Another red ping flared, and another, from
drones all over the city. "And I think she's just the appetizer."
