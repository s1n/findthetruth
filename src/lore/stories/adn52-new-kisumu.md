## New Kisumu

The great and the good were all there, mingling, drinking, chatting—and more. About a hundred of
the most powerful people from across the SSL were inside the plush exec dome, estimated Kenyangi
as she circled the room, waiting for her PAD to ping.

There—by the huge, arced window framed with the filigree of red bougainvillea—a small group of
five men, two women, and a bioroid peering downward as they sipped bright cocktails.

"Bingo," said the remote feed in Kenyangi's ear, picking up from her optic implant. It was Mack,
an SSO-Weyland operative, from another building three klicks north.

Not many places you got to see *two* SSL presidents *and* a PM, all in the same room. Soufrika,
Nigeria, and Uganda—all the key leaders driving the African Space Elevator Initiative were there.
They had all excluded SSO from the private partnership tender, *not* something the SSO leadership
were pleased with.

Kenyangi joined the group casually and stared down at the bobbing, giant platform on the shore of
Lake Victoria sixteen stories below them.

Even from this height, it still looked huge.

*Damn, you've got to admire it, the seed of the second Earth Station.* Her father, bless his
ancestral soul, had always been in favor of Africa reaching for the skies.

What she planned to do would disappoint him. Still, a girl had to make a living.

Kenyangi glanced up. The bioroid stood on the edge of the powerful group, watching her closely,
weaponed arms cocked.

This was *not* going to be easy.

Where the hell was he?

As if on cue, her PAD chimed, directing her gaze to a middle-aged man dressed in a colorful
dashiki, stalking toward her through the milling throng. The man bowed, offering her one of two
garish rainbow cocktails in his hands: "My name is Jonathan Mboob, aide to Her Excellency
Olatunji of the Nigeria Federation."

"Dr. Constance Makumbi," she replied, "a PhD from Zambia in African political physics. Thank you
for helping me."

"Two minutes," he said. "That's all, to aid your postdoc research, Dr. Makumbi."

She nodded gratefully. Two minutes was all it would take.

Jonathan Mboob was a helpful, friendly man, by all accounts. Also, the weakest link in the chain,
according to SSO intel. It never pays to be *too* nice, not nowadays.

Kenyangi took a sip, having sniffed it was safe with her nasal implants: marula and baobab liqueur
with a sprinkling of neural enhancers to perk up the party. She followed Mr. Mboob back to the
circle of power, still deep in earnest conversation.

Kenyangi caught the tail end of the conversation as Jon pulled her into the group.

"This structure will be a sign to the world of the rising might of Africa. We are not to be
trifled with."

Madam Olatunji was the one who had spoken, and she turned toward Kenyangi as Jonathan Mboob
introduced them. Kenyangi remembered at the last minute to shift her drink to her left hand. The
Nigeria Federation PM was majestic in her robes, taking Kenyangi's right hand with gentle
courtesy. "Good to meet you, Dr. Makumbi."

Jonathan introduced her next to President Malema from Soufrika and then the Ugandan PM, Nalule,
whose old yet gentle face and warm voice so reminded Kenyangi of her father.

Father, who had always worked hard and only wished for her to become a nuclear engineer.

She bowed in response, handing her drink to Jon.

"Just one question," stated Olatunji.

"Where will your extra funding for the space elevator come from, Your Excellency?"

Olatunji glanced at the Ugandan PM, but not at the the Soufrikan President. Still no love lost
between those two countries, given their previous war. The SSO was looking to fracture fragile
alliances, too, in its search to disrupt the current public-private partnership for the second
Beanstalk.

"It's no secret we are looking to our old relationship with New Beijing," said Madam Olatunji. "It
was good to meet you, Dr. Makumbi."

Now, hands free, and while everyone readied themselves for a farewell ritual, Kenyangi could seal
her nasal implants and activate the long-acting but deadly airborne toxin with a click of her
false left thumbnail. Just one click—and a day later they would all start to die.

Kenyangi bowed and primed her thumb for release. She caught a glimpse of PM Nalule's face. He was
smiling at her.

"No, my little egret." The voice came from the air above her.

"Father?" she asked, freezing the movement in her hand.

She caught the puzzled looks all around her. No one else had heard anything, it seemed. It was
then that she sensed she was being watched by someone—or something—else.

Kenyangi turned slowly to look beyond the edge of the group, expecting to see the bioroid from
earlier.

The humanoid drone facing her instead was short, suave, smooth-faced—and deadly.

"Tokoloshe!" rang in her ear.

It had been closely watching all this time.

"We are not to be trifled with," said the tokoloshe. Kenyangi did not bother to scan the room for
the remote operator.

Intel alone would have to be enough for the SSO this time.

Kenyangi walked away, knowing she was being filmed closely for any signs of suspicious activity.

She was going to need a new face. *Again…*

"Father?" She called again, into the noisy chatter of the air.

At the far side of the window, she leaned against the glass and began to breathe more freely.

Could it have been the neural enhancers? Whatever it was, it had saved her.

"Thank you, Father," Kenyangi whispered, looking down at the massive floating platform that he
would have been thrilled to see, had he lived long enough.

"Try again next time," said Mack in her ear.

Kenyangi, Africa's deadliest assassin, was no longer so sure there *would* be a next time.

"Let us Africans fly space," she said.

It had been her father's favorite phrase.
