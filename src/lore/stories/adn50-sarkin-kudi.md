## Sarkin Kudi

Lewi was new in town, but you wouldn't think it.

He lounged at an outside table of the Nalubaale District Bean Store, mirrorshades drinking in the
view on the square. Despite the late afternoon heat and his stiff blue smart-thread suit, not a
drop of sweat marred his face. He coolly sipped a vanilla chai and adjusted the zoom on his
lenses. ZeeBand—only the best for Lewi. A king's ransom, if there had been any kings left.

The steamy clouds overhead threatened to drop their daily rainy-season dose, but the man had
people nearby willing to offer him cover with an umbrella—or a machine gun.

The square bustled with commuters and traders, hustlers and gamers, all en route to home or town
central, depending on their business. The brown brick building across the way was small and
unassuming, yet a barely visible yellow electric shield buzzed across the door. An admin office
for publicly available patents, ostensibly.

So, the million-credit question: why the disguised, heavy security? Was SSO Industries posing as
the local council? And if so, what was it hiding in plain sight?

If rumors on the Shadow Net were true, it could have been data secured in datafortresses by its
parent company, Weyland—data on buckyweave, the very stuff used to support the space elevator in
New Angeles but as yet unavailable here, because SSO had failed to get *this* space elevator
contract.

Now *that* information would be priceless in a city looking to launch the second elevator into
the heavens over Lake Victoria.

Lewi was Senegalese and had learned to sharpen his unorthodox business sensibilities to
complement his natural talent for both making and smelling money. Kampala had called to him with
its growing stink.

Lewi's PAD earwigged him. Seventeen hundred hours. He took a last sip of chai. Time for things to
kick off…

Kintu, his employee, approached the door to the brown brick building dressed as a maize seller,
balancing cobs in a basket on her head.

"Plug in, Kintu!" Lewi muttered, closing his left eye.

The right lens faded, turning opaque grey momentarily. The Net link in his right ear kicked into
action. His gaze and hearing became hers.

"Let me in!" Kintu's voice was urgent and insistent, and as Lewi's lenses picked up her vision
from her cybereyes, it was as though a big burly man were wrestling with *him*.

Lewi retracted his involuntary strike at the man, which had propelled his tea off the table and
into a distracting crash on the floor.

"This is a council office, you clot!" The man stepped back, grimacing in pain. Lewi wasn't sure
where Kintu had hit him. "No one inside wants your pestilent maize!" the man barked, bristling in
his all-too-casual clothes.

Kintu looked down, her right arm briefly obscuring Lewi's view as she secured the basket balanced
on her head to prevent any more cobs from falling. About six or seven cobs lay on the red-tiled
veranda floor.

"I'll have you for assault," she said. "Now, pick up the goods you've made me spill."

"No," said the man, with a brief and surreptitious rub of his privates—Kintu had always had good
aim. "You're not wanted. Leave."

Kintu stared up at the man. "I can go where I want. Move out of my way."

The man glared down at her. "No more bullshit! Get on your way, before…"

The man fingered the palm of his right hand. An alarm—reinforcements being summoned from inside?

"What's going on here?" The harsh, abrupt voice came from behind.

Kintu spun around and Lewi caught a glimpse of another maize cob spiraling off her head.

Two bioroids stood behind each shoulder of a police officer, who was tapping her PAD
threateningly.

"Officer," insisted Kintu, "I demand to exercise my rights!"

The officer in her stiff blue uniform was not looking at Kintu, but at the man standing behind
her. "Is she a problem, sir?"

"Yes, officer. Please charge her with assault and remove her, immediately!"

So he had a line straight to the police? Surprise, surprise…

"That's a lie!" Kintu raised her voice, her anger cutting into his ear. "This man assaulted me
first, when I was only trying to—"

Kintu swiveled her gaze as the officer stepped aside with a bored expression to tap on her PAD.
She was not listening to Kintu. What *was* she doing?

One of the ASA bioroids stepped forward, handcuffs raised. "Citizen, we are arresting you for
causing a public disturbance. You have the right—"

But Lewi did not catch the words that came next. Despite himself, he swore and stood up abruptly.
"Cut Kintu link!"

His right eye lurched from a glimpse of falling maize cobs and basket to a view of a seller woman
in bright-orange cloth being handcuffed by a bioroid. The bouncer lurked in the background, arms
crossed in satisfaction.

"Replay scene, left lens," Lewi muttered. On his lens, he could trace the unmistakable flash of
digital transmission between the policewoman and the arresting bioroid. Kintu would demand a
shedload of extra, once they bailed her.

Lewi whistled. Seriously expensive shit, that: droning harked back to the Colony Wars on Mars.

Lewi's five bodyguards hovered behind him. He waved them still and swore again.

Surprise, followed by shock—no more tea left to drink, either.

The heavens opened with hot rain, and his leading bodyguard whipped out an umbrella with an LG
motif.

Lewi Guilhermi was indeed in town, and he'd wipe the smug grin off that bouncer just for
starters.

After that, Kampala itself!
