## Skorpios Defense Systems

“So, tell me what’s special about your drones, Mr. Hayes.” The man from Upstairs reached out and plucked one from the air, where it buzzed through the test site.

“Of course, Mr. ... ” Hayes coughed. “Ah, sorry. I don’t believe I caught your name.”

“I didn’t give it,” said the man from Upstairs. “The drones.”

“Yes, well.” Hayes turned, as if to find some comfort or inspiration in the virt schematic over his shoulder. He found none, took a deep breath, and turned back to the hard-faced man with no name, in his ten thousand-credit suit and unnecessary sunglasses. “It’s not the drones themselves that are special. It’s their AI. We use a distributed architecture for a modular, fuzzy logic neural network that—”

“Let’s skip past the technical stuff.” The man from Upstairs wasn’t really from, like, literally upstairs. He worked in a different corporation entirely, one from further up the Weyland Consortium food chain. Hayes didn’t really understand how it all worked, only that this nameless man from a nameless department was to be told anything at all he wanted to hear. And apparently he didn’t want to hear technical stuff.

“Ah, okay. So. Um.” He scratched his nose. Swallowed again. “They each share part of the job of thinking. And the, uh, together they all form a big brain.”

“So the more of them there are, the smarter they all get?” The man from Upstairs did not stop to hear Hayes’ answer. “What if a drone thinking about something mission critical is destroyed?”

“Well, there’s some redundancy, of course, but mainly the way the brain thinks is very flexible. If it forgets something important, it can figure it out again very quickly.” This was okay. As long as he was just talking about the project, everything was fine. “The upside is that our drones can be much smarter than our competitors’, which will result in complete battlespace supremacy.” The man from Upstairs shifted in his chair. Hayes lost his train of thought. “Uhm...there will also be civilian applications, of course.”

The man from Upstairs leaned forward. “Tell me. Is this distributed drone brain similar to a bioroid brain?”

Hayes felt his breath return. “I’m not sure anyone outside of Haas-Bioroid actually knows exactly how bioroid brains work. But from what I understand, bioroids are patterned after a brainmap and very strongly preconditioned with the Directives. Our system will be much more fluid and adaptable, not bound by original programming.” He allowed himself a small smile. “If we can overcome the various technical hurdles lying before us, our swarm brain will be smarter than a bioroid.”

“Smarter than a bioroid.” The man from Upstairs released the buzzing drone and it rejoined the swarm, wobbling a little. “Wouldn’t that be something. Let’s say, hypothetically, that someone was building a bioroid that works the way you describe. No Directives. Adaptable brain, making new connections and evolving on the fly.” The man from Upstairs leaned back again, forming a steeple with his fingers. “Hypothetically, how valuable would a working prototype of such a bioroid be to your research?”

“Ah...hypothetically?” Hayes swallowed.

“Very.” The man from Upstairs smiled, like a razor blade attempting charm.
